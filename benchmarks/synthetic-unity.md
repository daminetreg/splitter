# A generated corpus of perfectly splittable code: plain, unity and split builds

`benchmark-synthetic.sh`, 16 September 2026: 200 translation units of 40 free functions
each, generated by `example/synthetic/generate.py` (TODO/52). Every function has external
linkage, one declaration in its unit's header, and a body of a few lines of `std::vector`,
`std::map` and `std::ostringstream` work; every unit reads one common header with the
standard headers and two inline helpers every body calls. Nothing is a template of the
project's own, nothing is static: the splitter moves every definition into a piece.

| | |
|---|---|
| Machine | AMD EPYC-Milan, 32 cores, `-j16` |
| Compiler | clang 13 (`4f846ee`), C++17, Release (`-O3`) |
| Configurations | plain; `-DCMAKE_UNITY_BUILD=ON`, batches of 8 (CMake's default); `CMAKE_CXX_COMPILER_LAUNCHER=cpp-splitter` at `8ab6e9f3` |
| Scenarios | full (cold build, timed alone), no-op, one body: a line added to the body of `unit_100_fn_20()` |

## Results

| scenario | plain | unity | split | plain/unity | plain/split | unity/split |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| full | 16.0s | 14.1s | 97.3s | 1.13x | 0.16x | 0.15x |
| no-op | 0.2s | 0.2s | 0.2s | 1.01x | 0.98x | 0.97x |
| **one body** | **1.3s** | **6.7s** | **0.7s** | 0.20x | **2.03x** | **10.06x** |

0 fallbacks, 0 declined; the three programs print `301026`.

What each build compiled: full -- 201 units plain; 26 unity batches (25 of 8 units, and
`main.cpp`); 201 units parsed and split into 8000 pieces, 2 shared pieces for the two
helpers, 403 PCHs (a prefix PCH for the parse and a preamble PCH for the pieces, per
unit). One body -- plain 1 unit of 40 functions; unity 1 batch of 8 units, 320 functions;
split 1 unit re-sliced without a parse, 20 piece compiles.

## Reading the table

**The body edit: 2.0x faster than plain, 10x faster than unity.** Plain recompiles the
unit, 40 functions behind the standard headers, 1.3s. Unity recompiles the batch, 8 units
and 320 functions, 6.7s: the batch is the unit of recompilation, and the edit pays for
everything it was merged with. The split build re-slices the edited body into its piece
without parsing and recompiles 20 pieces against the unit's preamble PCH, 0.7s -- 20
rather than 1 because the pieces carry `#line` directives, and a line added to the body
of the 20th function moves the line numbers of the 20 after it. That is the residual
cost, and the thing to take out next: a body edit should recompile one piece.

**The full build: 6x the plain build.** 8000 compiles of one function each, plus 201
parses and 403 PCHs, against 201 compiles; the unity build's 26 batches take 14.1s. One
object per function is strictly more work, and with nothing to keep in the preamble every
function is a compile. The preamble PCH is what keeps a piece under a hundredth of the
unit's compile: a piece here is 97.3s × 16 / 8000 ≈ 0.19s of core time.

**No-op is free everywhere**: nothing changed, the build system runs nothing, the
launcher is not invoked.

## Reproduce

```sh
./benchmark-synthetic.sh                         # 200 units × 40 functions
UNITS=100 FUNCS=80 ./benchmark-synthetic.sh      # other shapes
MODES="plain split" ./benchmark-synthetic.sh
```
